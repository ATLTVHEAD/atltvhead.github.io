# Skating controller options: momentum, contact and continuous transitions

Research date: **2026-11-11**. Decision **CONTROLLER-01 — OPEN**.

**PROPOSED recommendation:** favor an **authored velocity-based arcade controller using a physics library for collision queries and, where suitable, kinematic move-and-slide** (B). Compare it against a genuinely redesigned custom-contact controller (A), not merely the current controller with different constants. A dynamic-body motor (C) remains credible, but later player collisions/ragdolls do not justify choosing it now. **No engine, body representation, interface, contact algorithm or implementation milestone is selected.**

The central question is **who owns trajectory and contact response**. An engine can supply body-shaped sweeps, contact normals and collision resolution; it does not supply this game's momentum, deliberate Grind/Wall ride engagement, forgiving assistance, Boost or combo rules. This conclusion is an engineering inference from the official controller/body contracts [R1–R7, J1–J3], not a claim about the undocumented implementations of *Air Gear* or *Bomb Rush Cyberfunk*.

## 3. What engines supply—and what they do not

### Kinematic control is not dynamic-body control

**Verified Rapier JS 0.21 contracts:** dynamic bodies respond to forces/contacts. Position-based kinematics receive a next pose; velocity-based kinematics receive a velocity. **Neither kinematic kind is stopped by contact forces**: obstacle avoidance is application queries or a character controller [R2]. “Velocity-based” in this report's arcade options means storing directional momentum; it does **not** itself select `KinematicVelocityBased` or a dynamic body.

Rapier's kinematic character controller computes corrected **translation**, exposes computed movement/grounding and chronological collision information, and lets the application apply the result. Gravity is application-owned. It supports slopes, optional steps/snap and optional impulses to dynamic objects, but **not rotational movement**; its documentation explicitly warns that a generic controller may not fit every game [R1, R4]. **Inference:** it is a candidate geometric service, not a skating motor or a replacement for mode-transition rules. A visual wall lean need not rotate the collider; actually rotating a non-symmetric collision body introduces a different clearance problem.

Rapier scene queries distinguish a point ray from a **whole shape translated along a straight line**. Shape casts report hit time, points and normals; queries can filter colliders [R3]. **Inference:** a sweep is not a complete multi-contact move-and-slide, penetration recovery or curved/rotating trajectory solver. Repeated casts/residual movement, query freshness, corner handling and support policy remain responsibilities unless a selected higher-level service demonstrably covers them.

**Material alternative: Jolt CharacterVirtual.** Its core source describes a query-driven character, not a rigid-body character, with explicit application updates; an optional inner rigid body supplies visibility to other physics queries/objects. Gravity must still be applied to character velocity by the application. Contacts distinguish predictive versus actual hits and allow overriding sliding velocity. JS bindings expose velocity, ground normal/velocity, contact callbacks, `Update`/`ExtendedUpdate`, floor sticking and stair operations [J1–J3]. The official JS example separately authors inertia, air input, Jump and gravity [J3]. This is useful corroboration of B's division of responsibility—not evidence of skating feel or a reason to copy demo movement/cleanup.

**Dynamic-body services:** Rapier supplies force/impulse integration, contact response with friction/restitution, rotation locks and joints [R5–R7]. Its PID controller is an example of driving a dynamic body toward a target by velocity correction [R1]. **Inference:** dynamic does not mean uncontrolled, but an aggressive motor can overwrite contact response so thoroughly that it recreates kinematic responsibilities with additional solver state. Conversely, leaving ordinary friction/rotation unconstrained does not guarantee controllable skating.

### Geometry correctness is still a project responsibility

Rapier supports primitive/convex shapes and fixed-environment triangle meshes; its guide warns that mesh triangles have no thickness/interior, thin long triangles can reduce stability, and dynamic non-convex mesh bodies are discouraged in favor of convex decomposition. It documents an internal-edge correction flag [R6]. Jolt documents padding, penetration recovery, predictive-contact distances and enhanced internal-edge removal [J1]. **Inference:** an engine helps cover body-sized side/ceiling collision but does not make arbitrary render triangles robust skating surfaces. Proxy continuity, gaps, internal-edge behavior, back-face/underside policy, shape margins and semantics still require representative checks.

Rapier's current CCD guide describes motion-clamping sweeps, additional cost/substep tradeoffs and remaining moving-body tunneling limitations [R8]. **Inference:** neither “CCD enabled” nor fixed 60 Hz establishes safe Boost speeds, all-corner correctness or a performance budget. Kinematic/query motion needs its own swept handling; a discrete overlap at the destination is not equivalent to a sweep.

## 5. Required transition behavior under each approach

The experience outcomes here are APPROVED in §1. The mechanics below are **PROPOSED reasoning/choices**, not approved formulas, state schemas or tuning values.

### Ordinary wall contact versus deliberate Wall ride

- For a static wall with outward unit normal `n` and incoming velocity `v`, an illustrative frictionless response removes only the into-wall component: `v' = v - min(dot(v,n), 0) * n`. This original mathematical illustration is **not a selected algorithm**. It shows why a glancing approach can retain tangential movement without zeroing all speed, and why normalizing the result back to full incoming speed would invent energy. Actual corners/multiple contacts, moving walls and support need more than one projection.
- Crash classification should consider **incoming** closing speed and incidence, before correction erases that evidence. High total speed alone must not turn every graze into a crash; nearly head-on low-speed contact should not automatically qualify as hard. Thresholds/hysteresis and brief recovery duration need user evaluation. One impact should not repeatedly break a combo every tick while touching the wall.
- **A** must compute reliable normals/hit timing and residual movement itself. **B** can obtain hit normals and corrected motion, but must reconcile stored velocity with valid contact response; blindly retaining the pre-impact velocity drives into the wall next tick. Blindly deriving all velocity from corrected displacement also risks turning snap/assist corrections into momentum. **C** has solver impulses/friction; the motor must not immediately restore the removed into-wall velocity. Low friction alone does not define head-on stumble or a satisfying along-wall policy [R1–R6].
- Wall slide stays ordinary traversal. Wall ride additionally requires deliberate intent, eligible authored wall/contact and bounded assistance; collision alone never engages it. Assistance must not pull the skater through a corner/obstacle or convert an ineligible surface into a trick. Exact engage mapping, hold/toggle/edge behavior and eligibility boundary remain OPEN.

### Curved slopes, natural takeoff and deliberate Jump

- All options need a coherent relationship between ground-relative motion, support normal and free-world velocity. Merely setting Y to a support height while retaining an X/Z heading repeats the current separation. Authored slope acceleration/grip and outgoing **3D tangent velocity** can make ramp shape and approach speed matter without simulating individual skate wheels.
- At an open lip, carry the outgoing motion into air rather than resetting vertical speed to zero. A rounded convex crest raises a harder choice: follow it with bounded arcade adhesion, or detach when maintaining contact would require more adhesion than allowed? Geometric loss-of-support alone and a curvature/speed-aware rule are different designs. **No curvature formula/threshold or adhesion model is selected.** These choices determine whether high-speed crests launch and low-speed rollovers remain grounded.
- **A** owns support continuity/detachment. **B** must deliberately govern generic snap-to-ground/step/slope behavior: Rapier snap can teleport a previously grounded character downward within its configured distance, and generic slope limits can reject a steep but intentionally skateable transition [R1]. Jolt likewise exposes floor-stick/stair operations and ground data separately [J1–J3]. Neither is a natural-ramp takeoff guarantee. Do not rotate the generic “up” vector at every wall contact and call it Wall ride.
- **C** gets dynamic gravity/contact integration, but a capsule's contact/friction and any hover/adhesion motor are not rollerblade wheels. Smooth takeoff is a hypothesis to test, not an automatic result of dynamics [R2,R5–R6]. Excessive ground adhesion can suppress takeoff here too.
- Deliberate Jump must have one defined relation to natural tangent carry—e.g. preserve it and add bounded lift, versus a controlled takeoff override. That choice and buffering/grounded reacquisition need approval. Preserve the historical protection against double-counted marked impulses unless explicitly superseded; do not silently stack a full marker launch, Jump and natural lift.

### Body sweeps, undersides and multi-level support

For every option, evaluate the **whole physical proxy**, not only the foot/contact point. A side scrape, upward underside hit and descending deck landing have different responses; an underside hit must not land on top of that deck. A roof is only support when the collision/semantic contract says so. Swept approach, valid support normal/contact and tolerances must establish a lawful landing; nearest-height alone cannot. For A this is custom geometry work; for B engine casts/contacts provide evidence; for C collision integration provides evidence, but the game's grounded/landed classification remains authored [R1–R3,R6,J1].

An upright capsule/rounded proxy with a separate leaning/animated visual root is a plausible **PROPOSED** starting comparison, not approved body geometry. It avoids making visual spins change doorway clearance accidentally. Leaning/rotating the physical proxy, crouch clearance, scale conversion and skate-foot contact offsets are significant alternatives. Rapier's translational controller explicitly does not validate rotational movement [R1].

### Grind capture, path travel and drop/Jump exits

- Keep semantic rail/ledge paths distinct from solids and capture assistance. A forgiving capture neighborhood is not an invisible walkable deck. Deliberate input, approach direction/speed, clearance and eligibility determine entry; automatic balance removes the current correction/failure game.
- **A/B** can author constrained path travel with signed distance and collision-safe body motion, then emit outgoing full tangent velocity at an endpoint/drop; Jump modifies that exit once. Time before capture and after exit must not be charged twice or discarded. The existing dismount's remaining-time idea is useful, while its downstream lower-floor fall bug is not (`grind-state.js:112–141,291–332`; `planar-skater.js:161–192`).
- A curved path sampled at large steps cannot safely be treated as one unobstructed endpoint teleport or one chord through a corner. Path representation/sampling accuracy, contact sweeps and allowed assist displacement need validation; this is not a selection of spline, substep count or integration algorithm.
- **C** can use dynamic constraints or a constrained motor, but “add a joint” is not a complete curved-grind implementation. For example, Rapier's prismatic joint permits translation along one axis, not an arbitrary bent rail [R7]. Stiff correction, solver contact and boost can fight; switching to kinematic/path control during tricks is possible but adds an explicit dynamic↔kinematic velocity/ownership handoff. That hybrid must be evaluated as such, not called continuous dynamics by default.

### Air control, Wall ride loss and Boost

- Air steering should be a bounded modification of existing velocity, not reassigning all momentum to facing. Visual yaw/tricks and trajectory changes are separate. A/B can author bounded acceleration/turn response; C can use bounded forces/impulses or a motor [R5]. Exact budget, speed dependence and direction under Boost remain OPEN. No option should restore today's identical ground/air steering branch by accident.
- Wall ride is a semantic traversal constraint/assist, not ordinary Coulomb friction. It carries along-wall momentum while imposing gradual downward travel; Jump releases with an outward component and useful carried velocity. Corner continuity, contact loss, low-speed exit and eligibility limits need explicit evaluation. B must govern generic sliding/slope restrictions; C needs adhesion/motor arbitration rather than hoping friction creates the chosen behavior [R1–R2,R6].
- Boost must feed the chosen motion model in **all four** travel situations. The game owns reserve spending/refill, stopped depletion, combo grace and Bomb Rush activation/duration. In A/B it can be bounded acceleration/speed shaping along the permitted direction. In C forces affect acceleration and impulses affect velocity, with mass dependence; Rapier's added forces persist until cleared, so Boost release needs correct force ownership/reset [R5]. These are not interchangeable APIs.
- A global `maxSpeed=12` clamp would erase the required extended speed envelope. Conversely, restoring a target velocity every tick can erase collisions, gravity and takeoff. Caps/diminishing acceleration/overspeed carry are unresolved tuning-policy choices. During Wall ride, Boost must extend tangential travel **without eliminating net height loss**; it must not silently act as hover/lift, including on slightly tilted walls. Specify that behavior before choosing the force direction or constraint math.

## 7. Future physics without overbuilding the initial controller

**PROPOSED inference:** leave a real boundary between gameplay intent/semantic traversal and collision observations, but do not construct a general physics abstraction, plugin system, ECS, rollback stack or ragdoll framework now.

- **Dynamic props later:** A can acquire engine queries/interactions later; B can interact with dynamic objects with an explicit push/carry policy; C starts with reciprocal physical contact. Rapier's KCC can optionally apply impulses to dynamic bodies, but the kinematic character still does not become a dynamic body [R1–R2]. Jolt's virtual character similarly exposes push settings/optional inner-body presence [J1–J2]. Neither guarantees the chosen moving-platform/crush/impact behavior.
- **Player collisions later:** mutual response across latency, local responsiveness, remote interpolation and who decides outcomes are separate gameplay/authority decisions. Merely adding colliders to remote rendered poses is not an approved collision architecture. The initial shared-world requirement remains; choosing B does not defer it behind solo polishing or challenges.
- **Ragdolls later:** articulated joints are engine services [R7], not an argument that ordinary skating must be a dynamic ragdoll. A/B locomotion could hand pose/velocity to a separately designed crash presentation/simulation later. Physical versus visual ragdoll, collision effects and recovery ownership are new decisions, not guaranteed drop-in features.
- **Capacity:** 12–16 is the requested initial validation cohort, not `WORLD_LIMITS.maxGuests` as a cap (`js/game/shared/world-config.js:11–14`; foundation audit §1). No candidate has been validated at that count. Future Playtest bots should help discover limits, not fabricate a scalability conclusion now.

## 8. Recommendation, assumptions and approval boundaries

**CONTROLLER-01 remains OPEN. PROPOSED recommendation: B**, contingent on a small comparison showing that engine contact services help rather than fight the required flow. Author the skating motor/transitions deliberately; use a library for geometry where it earns its dependency/lifecycle cost. **Rapier is an example candidate, not a selection.** Jolt CharacterVirtual materially illustrates another way to provide velocity/contact services, at the cost of explicit WebIDL/ref-count ownership and its own integration surface [J1–J3]. No ranking based on popularity or unmeasured speed is offered.

**Why B rather than immediately A or C:** the inspected code's largest contact gaps are general shape/side/underside/multi-level correctness, while the desired feel is intentionally nonphysical and tightly authored. B can address both concerns without requiring dynamic skates to simulate every transition. This is a reasoned hypothesis, not proof. A remains attractive if the user prefers no added engine and accepts a deliberately constrained proxy/geometry contract and its contact-maintenance burden. C gains weight if reciprocal dynamic interactions become initial gameplay, or a hands-on comparison reveals substantially better ramp/impact flow without excessive corrective logic.

**Assumptions/uncertainties:** static authored District initially; no initial player impacts/ragdolls; tolerable physics payload/startup cost yet to be measured; body proxy can differ from visual pose; semantic paths/walls remain authored; no requirement for lockstep/bitwise replay. If these assumptions fail, revisit the recommendation rather than quietly expanding scope. A KCC that cannot support steep curved ramps/continuity without deep work may make B's query-only form, A, or C preferable. No official source proves the desired feel on this content/hardware.

## 9. Small user-evaluated experiments — proposals only

These are **2–4 comparison ideas, not an implementation plan**. None has been run or authorized to build. Reuse the same shapes, inputs and camera assumptions so a comparison exposes controller differences rather than differing content polish. Desktop/gamepad hands-on review comes first.

1. **Ramp/crest/underpass comparison:** one curved rise with an open lip, one rounded rollover and a lower landing beneath a deck. Try low/ordinary/boosted approach speeds with and without Jump. User judges natural release, carried trajectory, underside clearance and trustworthy landings; numerical observations check no below-deck snap or loss of accumulated falling displacement. Compare A/B first; include C if uncertainty remains specifically about dynamic takeoff. Distinguish sampled geometry artifacts from controller policy.
2. **Wall-contact discrimination:** a straight wall, shallow graze, oblique hit, hard head-on approach and inside corner. Compare useful tangential travel, no corner speed creation, one brief crash response, and no automatic Wall ride. Then deliberately engage the eligible wall, Boost and Jump away: user checks assistance/readability, ongoing height loss and carried exit momentum.
3. **One connected transfer loop:** slope → air → deliberate curved Grind → drop/Jump → deliberate Wall ride → landing, with an ordinary-skating alternative. User judges automatic balance, bounded capture, air steering, useful velocity through exits, Boost/reserve readability and opportunities within the brief combo grace. Inspect timing/contact/event continuity rather than merely seeing all mode names occur. No need to polish a whole District or implement duels/ragdolls to answer this question.
4. **Browser/lifecycle cost comparison:** only if an engine candidate merits further consideration, compare its selected distributable on the actual static-serving path: cold/warm readiness, failure/retry, leave/rejoin, resource release and observed frame cost on agreed desktop hardware, then a representative phone. This tests dependency cost separately from subjective feel; it is **not** a claimed benchmark, hosting change or 12–16-client validation. Multi-client/shared Tagging validation remains necessary separately.
